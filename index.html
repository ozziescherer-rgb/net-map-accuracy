Net Map Accuracy: transformer intelligence from the AMI data utilities already have

Correcting meter-to-transformer records from ordinary smart-meter voltage data — verification-gated so corrections stop before they do net harm — then turning the corrected map into transformer aging rankings and EV hosting capacity.

The problem

Most utilities' records of which transformer serves which meter are somewhere between 5 and 20% wrong — ComEd's field audit documented errors on roughly 5% of 4 million meters. A wrong map quietly breaks every per-transformer analysis built on it, exactly as EV and heat-pump load concentrates on individual units with year-plus replacement lead times. Fixing the map manually costs $50–100+ per transformer visit, so it almost never happens.

The method

Meters sharing a transformer share a secondary, so their voltages move together. The method identifies mis-mapped meters from 15-minute AMI voltage correlation, proposes corrections ranked by confidence, and — the central contribution — stops applying them at the point where they would start doing harm, located empirically by about 40 crew spot-checks. Correction tools evaluated only on detection accuracy can leave a map worse than they found it; this one is evaluated on net map accuracy after application, and gated on it.

~93%of fixable record errors corrected (benchmark on EPRI utility-derived test feeders)
~55%published state of the art on the same task

No net harmat realistic error rates (≤10%), the gated map is never left worse than the records it started from

The corrected map then feeds IEEE C57.91 thermal models: which transformers are aging fastest, and how much more load each one can take.

What it's worth — example: a 15,000-transformer system

$750k–1.5Mcost of getting the same corrected map by manual field verification at standard truck-roll rates ($50–100+ per transformer) — this method needs ~40 checks instead
$25–75k/yrestimated avoided emergency replacements: the corrected map surfaces at-risk units the wrong map hides (in benchmarks, top-25 at-risk capture improves from 14 to 19), turning $8–15k emergency failures into planned work
Capex targetingper-transformer headroom lets EV/heat-pump-driven upgrades go where they're needed instead of blanket upsizing — at today's transformer prices and lead times, the largest and least quantifiable saving

Figures are order-of-magnitude, from standard industry labor and equipment costs plus benchmark results; the free field validation exists precisely so a utility can check them against its own numbers.

Looking for a first field site

The trade: a utility provides an anonymized AMI extract (pseudonymous IDs — no names, addresses, or account data) and roughly 40 field spot-checks by its crews, at whatever pace works. It keeps the corrected connectivity map, the transformer aging ranking, and the per-transformer hosting-capacity analysis. No cost, no hardware, no integration — the export is about an afternoon of one admin's time.
